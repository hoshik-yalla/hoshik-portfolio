Career forecast

Most of what I've built so far shares one constraint: it has to work when the easy assumptions don't hold. A medical chatbot for astronauts with no reliable link to Earth. A sign-language model light enough to run in a browser instead of a lab. An automation tool sized for a nonprofit's budget, not a Fortune 500 IT department.

I'm heading toward a computer science or robotics program where I can keep building for that middle ground — applied AI and local, resource-efficient systems — rather than research that only works with unlimited compute. Long term, I want to be the engineer teams call when the deployment environment is the hard part of the problem.

Mission statement

I build tools for people who are usually left out of the pitch deck — astronauts without a signal, students without lab equipment, immigrant families without a guide to a new system. My job is to make something useful while assuming as little as possible about what the user already has.

My parents both went through university in India, not the U.S., which made the American college process something I've largely had to map out myself. It's the same instinct behind everything else I build: when there's no clear path, make one, and leave it better documented for whoever's behind you.
